FINDING (closed-negative — a deterministically ruled-out axis):
  The arithmetic-intensity lever is REFUTED for this conv-MoE kernel. Higher FLOP/byte (wider
  BM·BN, fatter TM×TN) MONOTONICALLY REGRESSES throughput, and the highest-intensity config is
  the worst. Mechanism (the predicted W12/OG17 failure mode, now CONFIRMED here):
    • Wider tile → bigger register accumulator (TM×TN up to 8×8=64 acc/thread) + bigger smem
      (Ws[K][BK][BN] grows with BN) → fewer CTAs resident / fewer warps per SM → OCCUPANCY
      collapses. H100 needs many in-flight warps to hide the WEIGHT-stream HBM latency; the
      wide tile trades exactly that latency-hiding parallelism for on-paper intensity.
    • K=3, BK=16 is a SHALLOW contraction inner loop — the reuse a GEMM register-tile buys
      (amortising a deep K-loop over a fat micro-tile) is structurally absent here. The conv's
      weight bank is touched ~once (15.87GB streamed); there is no deep-K to amortise, so a
      wider tile adds register/smem pressure WITHOUT a compensating reuse win.
    • This is the SAME wall the cp.async PROD-PERF probe hit from the other side: the bound is
      weight/L2 BANDWIDTH (13.87GB weights / launch), and NEITHER latency-hiding (cp.async, path
      G) NOR arithmetic-intensity (wider tile, path H) can move a fundamentally bandwidth-bound,
      reuse-poor kernel toward the cuBLAS roofline.

  RULED OUT: the register-tile-width axis as a lever to close the E→cuBLAS gap at d=6208.
  STANDS: path E (64×64 t4×4) is the production deliverable — 6.57× over ModuleList @ H100, the
  byte-exact hexa-ownable cure. The remaining E→cuBLAS gap is a fundamental register-vs-bandwidth
