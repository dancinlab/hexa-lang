===================== CROSSOVER-D per dtype (consumer-card frontier) =====================
There is NO crossover-D where flame retakes the lead on the RTX 5070 — flame LOSES to
torch.compile at every D for every dtype. Instead the meaningful axis is the RATIO trend:

  TF32 : LOSE everywhere. Gap WIDENS with D at B=1 (1.78x@768 -> 8.96x@2048 — the small-M
         GEMM is launch/glue-bound and torch's inductor fuses the whole step into a couple
         kernels while flame still pays per-launch + a separate cuBLAS handle). At B=8 the
         gap is flatter (~2.7-2.9x) — bigger M amortizes flame's overhead somewhat but
         torch.compile still wins by ~3x.
  BF16 : LOSE everywhere, WORST dtype for flame. Ratio blows up to 14.66x at D=2048/B=1
         (torch's inductor + cuBLASLt BF16 path is extremely efficient on small-M; flame's
         per-step f32->bf16 cast kernels + cuBLAS overhead dominate the tiny matmul). B=8
         ratios 2.8-5.3x.
  FP64 : LOSE everywhere but CLOSE TO PARITY (ratio 1.04-1.32x), and TIGHTEST at large B:
         FP64/D=1536/B=8 = 1.007x (essentially tied), D=2048/B=8 = 1.038x. FP64 is
         rate-limited (compute-bound) on consumer Blackwell, so the GEMM dominates the step
         wall and flame's glue overhead is amortized -> flame nearly matches torch.

CONSUMER-CARD vs H100: on H100 the BENCH-10 fused step was competitive/winning in the
glue-bound regime; on the 5070 (fewer SMs, smaller L2, much weaker FP64/TF32 tensor rate
relative to its launch-overhead floor) torch.compile wins across the board. The earlier
BENCH-1 "flame won at D=768 on the 5070" claim does NOT reproduce here against this torch
2.12 eager+compile baseline — at D=768/B=1 torch eager is 0.21 ms vs flame 0.43 ms (TF32).

WHERE FLAME WINS ON CONSUMER HW: nowhere on raw step-rate in this sweep. Flame's standing
on the 5070 is "near-parity ONLY in FP64-compute-bound large-batch" (ratio ~1.0); its value
on consumer HW remains its IDENTITY properties (byte-exact / device-resident / deterministic /
no-LLVM / no torch dependency), NOT step throughput. For users who need raw speed on a
consumer card, torch.compile is faster everywhere; flame is the right tool when bit-exact
reproducibility or a torch-free native step matters more than the 1-9x speed delta.
