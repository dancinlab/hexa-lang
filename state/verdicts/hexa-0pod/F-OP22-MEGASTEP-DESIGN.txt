§2 — THE HONEST AMDAHL CEILING  (real cited numbers)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Amdahl for a perfect valley-fill (cited FF-DUTYCYCLE [VERDICT]):
  If GEMM is X% of wall and valley is (1−X)%, a PERFECT megakernel that filled
  the valley to zero caps at 1/X.
    X = GEMM% = 0.04% = 0.0004
    AMDAHL CEILING = 1/X = 1/0.0004 = 2844×   (FF-DUTYCYCLE: 2844.131×)

⚠ WHY 2844× IS A USELESS CEILING (the honest reading):
  The ceiling is astronomically large for the WRONG reason — GEMM is a rounding
  error, so 1/GEMM% blows up. A large 1/X does NOT imply a megakernel reaches
  it; Amdahl gives the LIMIT of a perfect serial-fraction removal, not what the
  megakernel architecture actually achieves. The achievable bound is governed
  by THREE real walls the megakernel does NOT remove:

  (W1) DEPENDENCY-SERIAL DAG. Every fwd phase grid.syncs on the previous
       (conv→GN→gelu→residual→router/experts→gelu2→pack→combine→GN2). The valley
       is serial by data dependence — fusing launches does not parallelize it.
       The practical lever is ≈ 1/duty-cycle ≈ 1/0.109 ≈ 9× from MEAN util
       (FF-DUTYCYCLE notes ceiling >> practical 1/duty ~9×), and even that is
       only reachable if the serialization itself is broken (it is not, by
       megakernel fusion alone).

  (W2) BYTE-EQ-FORCED SINGLE-THREAD GN. The dominant on-device cost (90.5%
       GPU-active) is the single-thread GN reduction. A megakernel that keeps
       byte-eq KEEPS this single-thread reduction (GN-GRIDSYNC: coop launch
       buys ZERO reduction parallelism). So the biggest active-time bucket is
       UNTOUCHED by fusion under byte-eq.

  (W3) OCCUPANCY FLOOR @ FP64/B=1. The cooperative grid (≈528–752 blocks, ONE
       persistent wave) UNDER-FILLS the H100's 132 SMs at B=1/D1536/T512; FP64
       register pressure lowers maxActiveBlocks. MEDIAN util 1–2% = device idle
       most of every step (F-FUSION-MEGASTEP §occupancy).

  NET ACHIEVABLE (cited, MEASURED — not theory): the literal whole-step
  megakernel realizes MEAN +3.4pp (10.4→13.8%), MEDIAN unmoved (1→1–2%),
  self-speedup ~1.0–1.04× (PRECISION-CHANGE 1.02–1.03×; M2 +3.4pp). That sits
  at the LOW end of even the bounded launch-elimination window the literature
  gives (Ada-MK 14.6%, CUDA-graph ~1.4×; reference_megastep_research), because
  the residual host glue + grid.sync barriers + serial DAG re-introduce the
  idle fusion was meant to remove. The 2844× Amdahl number and the ~1.0×
  measured reality are the two ends of the honesty gap.

§3 — THE MEGASTEP MEGAKERNEL DESIGN  (traced to the megakernel verdicts)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESIGN 1-LINE: a persistent grid-resident cooperative megakernel
(cudaLaunchCooperativeKernel, sized to ONE wave via
cudaOccupancyMaxActiveBlocksPerMultiprocessor × SM count) that runs the WHOLE
flame CLMConvMoE train step — embed→conv→GN→gelu→residual→router/experts→
gelu2→pack→softmax-combine→GN2→logits→CE→bwd-glue→cooperative-AdamW — as
in-kernel PHASES separated by cooperative_groups::this_grid().sync() barriers,
with the conv GEMMs as INLINE own-GEMM (replacing the un-fusable cuBLAS host
calls) so they CAN live inside the persistent kernel, host fully off the
critical path.

PHASE STRUCTURE (each phase = a grid.sync()-delimited block; all intermediates
device-resident across barriers — no relaunch gap). Traced to the as-built
kernels in F-FUSION-P1-TF32-MEGASTEP / M2 / FF-VALLEY:
  P0  embed (xe)                                          [_hx_k_embedding]
  P1  conv(xe→xec), conv(xec→h0)        inline own-GEMM   [im2col causal-dilated
                                                           folded into inner loop]
  --- grid.sync ---
  P2  groupnorm#1 (reduce in canonical thread → sync →    [_hx_k_groupnorm_coop;
      parallel normalize) → gelu → residual (xt)           GN-GRIDSYNC primitive]
  --- grid.sync ---
  P3  conv-router(xt→logits_r), conv-experts×2(xt→eo0/eo1) inline own-GEMM
  --- grid.sync ---
  P4  gelu2 → expert-pack → moe-router(softmax+combine)   [L3-d _hx_k_moe_block2,
      → groupnorm#2 (coop reduce→sync→normalize)           byte-eq order]
  --- grid.sync ---
  P5  conv(yn→out_logits)                                 inline own-GEMM
  P6  CE loss + softmax-grad seed (ce_grad)               [device-glue, L3]
  --- grid.sync ---
  P7  full bwd chain device-glue                          [conv_bwd · groupnorm_bwd
      (GN-bwd · gelu-bwd · moe-router-bwd · embed-scatter)  · moe_router_bwd ·
                                                            gelu_bwd · scatter-add]
  --- grid.sync ---
  P8  cooperative AdamW over all 17 params in ONE launch  [_hx_k_adamw_fused, M2
                                                            HEXA_CLM_FULLSTEP]
  build: nvcc -x cu -DHEXA_CUDA -rdc=true -arch=sm_90 + -dlink + -lcudadevrt
         (rdc REQUIRED for cooperative_groups grid.sync).

BOTH MEGAKERNEL WALLS ARE ALREADY CLOSED (cited F-FUSION-MEGAKERNEL-DESIGN
2026-06-06 UPDATE + GN-GRIDSYNC, real A100 sm_80):
  WALL 1 (own-GEMM): a persistent kernel calls the device _hx_k_gemm in-line
    instead of an un-fusable cuBLAS host call (own-GEMM stack #2697).
  WALL 2 (GN grid-sync): the 2 GroupNorm full-Y reductions fuse via
    _hx_k_groupnorm_coop + this_grid().sync() WHILE byte-eq max|Δ|=0 (A100
    4/4 cases incl. T=1536 C=1536 G=1 → max|Δ|=0, bitdiff_words=0; the canonical
    reducer thread runs the IDENTICAL sequential t-outer/c-inner accumulation,
    grid.sync broadcasts mu/inv, only the per-element normalize is parallel).
  => The glue-block whole-step megakernel is structurally REALIZABLE and
     cuBLAS-call-free. The design is buildable; it just does not WIN (§2/§5).

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE THREE HONEST TENSIONS (each cited, each load-bearing)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TENSION 1 — own-GEMM vs cuBLAS SPEED (the fusion-cost trade).
  To be IN-kernel, the conv GEMMs must use own-GEMM (a persistent kernel cannot
  nest a cuBLAS host launch). own-GEMM is ~6× off cuBLAS (TF32 W10 summit 70.7
  TFLOP/s, 6.09× off cuBLAS-TF32, F-FUSION-SM90-WGMMA-W10; FP16 W14 11.5× off).
  So the megakernel TRADES cuBLAS GEMM speed for fusion. At B=1 this trade is
  ~free ONLY because GEMM is 0.04% of wall (§1) — but it means the megakernel
  can NEVER recover the GEMM-bound regime (larger B / larger D), exactly where
  cuBLAS wins. The megakernel is a B=1-niche structure.

TENSION 2 — byte-eq ⊥ util-lift (mutually exclusive gate halves, MEASURED).
  A persistent kernel's inline own-GEMM accumulates k sequentially (one thread
  per output) ≠ cublasDgemm's tiled/blocked order → different IEEE-754 last
  bits. F-FUSION-B6-BYTEEQ-MEGAKERNEL (real pod): max|Δ first_ce| = 9.0e-16
  (D512) … 1.8e-15 (D1536) ≠ 0 AFTER ONE forward pass, before any training
  compounding — isolating the cause to GEMM REDUCTION ORDER. byte-eq max|Δ|=0
  vs cublasDgemm is STRUCTURALLY UNREACHABLE for own-GEMM-in-megakernel. You
  get byte-eq OR the megakernel, not both.

TENSION 3 — parity own-GEMM CANNOT co-reside (occupancy/deadlock, STRUCTURAL).
  F-FUSION-MEGA-OWNGEMM-INTEGRATE (real H100 sm_90a): wiring the high-throughput
  OG17 TF32-parity wgmma own-GEMM as the in-kernel GEMM is structurally
  IMPOSSIBLE at production CLM shapes on TWO axes:
    (a) wgmma needs a 128-thread warpgroup; the megakernel runs uniform
        blockDim=64 → blockDim<128 CANNOT issue wgmma (all-shapes structural).
    (b) the wgmma coop one-wave residency ceiling is 2 CTA/SM × 132 = 264 CTAs,
        but the GEMM output-tile grid scales (S/128)²: S=2048→256 fits,
        S=4096→1024 EXCEEDS the wave → grid.sync DEADLOCK.
  So a single-launch cuBLAS-free coop megakernel with a PARITY in-kernel GEMM is
  NOT achievable — the fast own-GEMM and the cooperative-residency requirement
  are in DIRECT structural conflict. The megakernel must use the SLOW
  (non-warpgroup) own-GEMM, deepening Tension 1.

GRID-SYNC RESIDUAL (cited F-FUSION-MEGAKERNEL-GN-GRIDSYNC): the coop GN closes
  the correctness wall but buys ZERO reduction parallelism — byte-eq forces the
  single-thread reduction, so the dominant on-device cost (§1, GN 90.5%) stays
  serial inside the megakernel. The grid.sync() PAIR (reduce→sync→normalize)
  makes GN fuse-able mid-megakernel; it does NOT make it FAST.

§6 — MEGASTEP vs TF32 (OP-20): THE HONEST DECISION
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
QUESTION (the task's headline): given OP-20's TF32 4.2× @B=1 0-pod win, is
MEGASTEP (a) still worth it (orthogonal gain on top of TF32) or (b) dominated?

ANSWER: (b) DOMINATED. Three independent reasons:

  1. SAME VALLEY, DIFFERENT TOOL. Both TF32-mode and MEGASTEP attack the
     between-GEMM valley. TF32 attacks it by making the GN/glue cheaper
     (parallel reduction, relaxed precision) AND letting GEMMs go inline
     CE-safely; MEGASTEP attacks it by removing launches. The valley is the
     SAME 99.96%, and the MEASURED outcomes are TF32 ~4.2× (sibling OP-20 /
     consistent with on-main P1-TF32 +5.5pp util, CE-safe descent) vs MEGASTEP
     ~1.0–1.04× self-speedup (M2 +3.4pp; PRECISION-CHANGE 1.02–1.03×). TF32 is
     ~4× the win at ~0 architecture risk.

  2. MEGASTEP'S BEST CASE IS BYTE-EQ-LOCKED, AND THAT IS THE TRAP. The only
     GREEN megakernel slice (FF-VALLEY 2.5×) is GREEN ONLY because byte-eq
     forces a pathologically-slow single-thread GN — i.e. its speedup is
     measured against an artificially-slow baseline. The moment you relax to
     TF32 (which OP-20 authorizes and CE-validates), GN becomes a parallel
     reduction in microseconds, the valley collapses to a few %, and the
     megakernel lever drops to the MPK ~1.2–1.3× cross-task-pipelining ceiling
     (FF-VALLEY caveat, verbatim). So MEGASTEP's headline number EVAPORATES in
     exactly the regime where you would actually run it.

  3. NO ORTHOGONAL STACK. Could MEGASTEP add gain ON TOP of TF32? The two are
     not additive at B=1: TF32 already pulls the GEMMs inline CE-safely and
     lifts util to 34.5% MEAN (P1-TF32), and the residual idle is the serial-DAG
     dependency floor (P1-TF32 FINDING: "the util ceiling is the serial-DAG idle
     floor, deeper than both precision and launch-fusion"). MEGASTEP removes
     LAUNCHES; but in the TF32 path the launches are already collapsed into the
     one cooperative megafwd. There is no separate launch-elimination win left
     for a byte-eq megakernel to add — and a byte-eq megakernel CANNOT co-exist
     with TF32 GEMMs anyway (different math). The honest residual lever past TF32
     is BATCH-FILL (≈3× asymptote, SM-saturation) and breaking the phase
     serialization (multi-step pipelining), NEITHER of which is "fuse the whole
     step into one byte-eq megakernel."

DECISION: do NOT spend an H100 campaign on MEGASTEP. It is already-measured
closed-negative (§2/§3 tensions) AND strictly dominated by the cheaper TF32
win (OP-20). Bank TF32 as the B=1 lever; pursue BATCH-FILL for SM-saturation;
treat MEGASTEP as a completeness artifact (100% hexa-owned, cuBLAS-call-free —
F-FUSION-MEGAKERNEL-DESIGN), NOT a perf path. The literature being
inference/decode-ONLY is now EXPLAINED, not just cited: a whole-train-step
megakernel under-fills the SMs at FP64/B=1, can't hold byte-eq, can't co-reside
a parity GEMM, and its launch-elim win is Amdahl-capped to a valley a cheaper
precision-change already drains.
