THE QUESTION (the critical test)
--------------------------------
Does a byte-wise ASCII upper/lower, applied to a UTF-8 string, wrongly
uppercase a CONTINUATION byte that happens to fall in 0x61-0x7A ('a'-'z'),
subtracting 0x20 → different bytes → MOJIBAKE for Korean / CJK / emoji?

DECISIVE FACT (UTF-8 structure):
  Every byte of a NON-ASCII codepoint — both the lead byte (>= 0xC0) and
  every continuation byte (0x80-0xBF) — has the high bit SET, i.e. is
  >= 0x80 = 128. The ASCII-letter ranges are 0x41-0x5A (65-90) and
  0x61-0x7A (97-122), ALL < 0x80. THEREFORE no byte of any multibyte UTF-8
  sequence can EVER land in an ASCII-letter range. A byte-wise fold that
  guards `97<=b<=122` (or `65<=b<=90`) and passes every other byte through
  is PROVABLY multibyte-SAFE: it can only touch genuine ASCII letters.
  Python oracle confirmed even café's é (0xC3 0xA9) and 한글
  (0xED 0x95 0x9C 0xEA 0xB8 0x80) contain NO byte in 0x61-0x7A.
