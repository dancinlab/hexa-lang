============================ ⚠ ISA FINDING: OG10 is sm_90a, aiden is sm_120 ============
OG10 (self/native/wgmma/wgmma_tf32_w10.cu) uses Hopper warpgroup-async MMA:
  wgmma.mma_async.sync.aligned.m64n64k8.f32.tf32.tf32 + wgmma.fence / commit_group /
  wait_group + cp.async.bulk.tensor (TMA). These are the sm_90a (Hopper) tensor-core ISA.

aiden = RTX 5070 = consumer Blackwell, sm_120 (cc 12.0). Compiling OG10 for sm_120 FAILS
at ptxas (CUDA 13.0.88, run ON aiden — the cheap ISA check, done FIRST):

  ptxas wgmma_tf32_w10.ptx, line 429: error : Instruction 'wgmma.fence' not supported
        on .target 'sm_120'
  ptxas wgmma_tf32_w10.ptx, line 468: error : Instruction 'wgmma.mma_async with floating
        point types' not supported on .target 'sm_120'
  ...(also wgmma.commit_group, wgmma.wait_group; ~22 wgmma sites total reject)...

→ HONEST sm_120-vs-sm_90a GAP: the Hopper wgmma async-warpgroup-MMA instruction family
  is NOT part of the consumer-Blackwell (sm_120) ISA. Blackwell tensor cores use a
  different encoding (tcgen05 / 5th-gen) — OG10-exact CANNOT run on aiden without a port.
  OG10's 70.7 TFLOP/s / bit-exact summit stands ONLY on sm_90a (Hopper, e.g. H100).

GEMM ACTUALLY USED (fallback per the BENCH-3 plan, option (a)): cuBLAS-TF32 as the
tuned-GEMM PROXY — cublasGemmEx with CUBLAS_COMPUTE_32F_FAST_TF32 +
CUBLAS_TF32_TENSOR_OP_MATH. This is the realistic CEILING a tuned/own-GEMM gives: a
sm_90a OG10 would APPROXIMATE cuBLAS-TF32 within its measured 6.09x off-cuBLAS factor,
so cuBLAS-TF32 here UPPER-BOUNDS how much OG10 would have closed the gap (OG10 would close
LESS, being 6.09x slower than cuBLAS). Both the NAIVE baseline (re-measured on the same
binary) and the cuBLAS proxy run from ONE source (tool/bench/flame_bench_step_og.cu,
-DGEMM_BACKEND=0 vs =1), identical step DAG to BENCH-1 (fwd GEMM → groupnorm+gelu valley →
bwd GEMM → AdamW).

================= HOW MUCH DID THE TUNED GEMM SHRINK THE BENCH-1 3-8x NAIVE GAP? =======
Headline (vs the PUBLISHED BENCH-1 best-torch÷naive ratios): the tuned/own-GEMM proxy
flattens the gap to a near batch-INVARIANT ~2.0-2.9x, where the naive gap GREW with batch:

   B | BENCH-1 NAIVE (published) | BENCH-3 cuBLAS-proxy | gap SHRANK by
   --+---------------------------+----------------------+--------------
   1 |          3.03x            |        2.20x         |    1.38x
   2 |          4.08x            |        2.25x         |    1.82x
   4 |          5.54x            |        2.07x         |    2.67x
   8 |          7.88x            |        2.85x         |    2.77x

  The naive GEMM's gap WIDENED with batch (3.03 → 7.88x) because the naive tiled kernel
  does NOT scale with the larger GEMM M (it under-uses the tensor cores torch saturates).
  The tuned GEMM REMOVES that batch-scaling penalty: the cuBLAS gap is flat ~2x and even
  IMPROVES to 2.07x at B=4. At B=8 the tuned-GEMM gap is 2.85x vs naive's 7.88x — a 2.77x
  shrink — the single biggest win, exactly where the naive kernel was worst.

  flame's own GEMM-only speedup (cuBLAS ÷ naive step/s): B1 1.43x · B2 1.81x · B4 2.45x ·
  B8 3.46x. The bigger the batch, the more the tuned GEMM helps — because the GEMM is a
  larger share of the step wall at high M.

============================ TORCH-PARITY REGIME? =====================================
NO. flame-with-tuned-GEMM reaches NO torch-parity regime in the TF32 lane: the residual
2.0-2.9x persists at every B. Why the floor doesn't close to 1x:
  (a) cuBLAS-TF32 is the PROXY ceiling; a real OG10 (6.09x off cuBLAS on its native
      sm_90a) would land FURTHER from torch, not closer — so 2.0-2.9x is the OPTIMISTIC
      bound, not the OG10-achievable number.
  (b) the residual is now the NON-GEMM glue (per-row groupnorm valley reduction +
      transpose + AdamW elementwise) + per-kernel launch overhead of the serial DAG,
      which torch.compile fuses/overlaps and flame's discrete-launch step does not.
  The GEMM was the batch-SCALING part of the gap (now removed); the flat ~2x floor is
  occupancy/launch/glue, a DIFFERENT lever (megakernel/graph-capture — closed-neg for
  byte-eq elsewhere). In the FP64 lane (BENCH-1) flame already WINS B>=4 (torch has no
  FP64 tensor-core path) — that remains flame's parity/win regime, unchanged by BENCH-3.

================================ HONEST BOTTOM LINE ===================================
A tuned/own-GEMM (cuBLAS-TF32 proxy) shrinks the BENCH-1 naive TF32 gap from a
batch-growing 3.03x→7.88x down to a flat ~2.0-2.9x (up to 2.77x shrink at B=8), by
removing the naive kernel's batch-scaling penalty. It does NOT reach torch parity — the
residual ~2x is launch/glue/occupancy, not GEMM. OG10-exact (the intended own-GEMM)
could NOT be measured here: it is sm_90a (Hopper wgmma) and ptxas rejects it for aiden's
sm_120 consumer-Blackwell ISA. cuBLAS-TF32 is the optimistic upper bound of OG10's
benefit (OG10 is 6.09x off cuBLAS, so would close the gap LESS). flame's value stays
byte-exact/deterministic (max|delta|=0 all cells) + its TF32-naive gap is now explained
and bounded; raw TF32 throughput remains torch's by ~2x even with a tuned GEMM. Free pool
GPU (aiden), zero vast, no leak.
