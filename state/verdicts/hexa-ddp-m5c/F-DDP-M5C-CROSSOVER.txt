F-DDP-M5C-CROSSOVER — HEXA-DDP DDP-M5c
NVLink crossover: the model size where multi-GPU DDP TRAINING beats 1-GPU.

STATUS: GREEN — N=4 data-parallel TRAINING byte-eq (1-GPU == 4-GPU, max|delta|=0,
FP64) on REAL NVLink hardware (canAccessPeer=1 on all 12 pairs, NV12 bonds), AND
per-step training wall measured at N=1/2/4 GPUs across a model-size sweep on BOTH
a NVLink A100 node and a staged-host A100 node (identical 4x A100-SXM4-40GB
silicon, differing ONLY in P2P transport — a controlled A/B). M5c TESTED the M5b
prediction that NVLink moves the crossover toward a smaller model: CONFIRMED in
DIRECTION (efficiency leaps from ~43%/18% staged -> ~50%/24% NVLink, i.e. NEAR-
IDEAL), but HONESTLY the crossover above 1.0x is NOT reached in the swept H range
on either node — 2-GPU asymptotes to 0.99x (just under) even on NVLink, because
the per-rank H^2 GEMM is weight-bound not batch-bound (sharding B=64 over N rows
barely shrinks per-rank compute when H is large), so DDP can APPROACH but not
exceed 1.0x for this data-parallel-only MLP. The NVLink effect is large and
measured: it removes essentially the ENTIRE comm tax (eff -> the ideal 50%/25%).

MODEL: MLP  D_IN=64 -> H -> H -> D_OUT=64  (ReLU,ReLU,linear), MSE loss, FP64.
  Global batch B=64 (split 64/32/16 across N=1/2/4 ranks). LR=0.01, SGD. H is
  the model-SIZE knob swept {512,1024,2048,2560,3072,3456}; NPARAM 328768 ->
  12,393,280. (H>=4096 exceeds the A100 167KB shared-mem optin cap of this
  kernel, so the swept ceiling is H=3456.) The DDP change vs the 1-GPU reference
  is ONLY (a) batch shard per rank + (b) grad SUM-reduce via the canonical ring
  all-reduce; all fwd/bwd math, init, target, LR, step are identical. SAME
  harness as DDP-M5b (stdlib/ddp/m5c_crossover/ddp_train_m5c.cu).

GATE (1) CORRECTNESS — 1-GPU W_ref == 4-GPU DDP W_ddp byte-eq (NVLink node, verbatim):
================= N=4 BYTE-EQ GATE (H=512) =================
loss_ref (mean)            = 13859.109009485161
grad  g_ddp vs g_ref  max|delta| = 0  BYTE-EQ
rank-agreement (Wr==W0) max|delta| = 0  ALL RANKS IDENTICAL
WEIGHTS W_ddp vs W_ref  max|delta| = 0  BYTE-EQ PASS

  -> max|delta| = 0 on the SUMMED grad, on every post-step WEIGHT, and across all
     4 ranks. This is the M4/M5b DDP invariant (mean-loss grad = SUM of per-sample
     grads / B; split B over W ranks, all-reduce-SUM the per-rank partials, /B on
     every rank => the exact 1-GPU grad) with the ring's per-chunk RIGHT-NESTED
     reduction tree (the only tree that drives the gate to a TRUE max|delta|=0 at
     N>=3 — FP add is non-associative). Identical max|delta|=0 on the staged node
     (transport-agnostic: the ring carries the same partials in the same order
     whether P2P is NVLink or host-staged). Correctness is independent of NVLink.

NVLink vs STAGED (the controlled A/B on identical 4x A100-SXM4-40GB silicon):
  NVLink removes essentially the ENTIRE comm tax. At every H, the NVLink per-step
  wall is at or near the 1-GPU wall (N=2 adds <1% at H>=2048; N=4 adds <3%),
  whereas the staged node pays 16-21% (N=2) and 35-45% (N=4) extra. Concretely at
  H=2048: NVLink 4-GPU = 48.90ms vs 1-GPU 47.37ms (1.03x slower); staged 4-GPU =
  65.37ms vs 47.36ms (1.38x slower). The efficiency lift is monotone and large:
     2-GPU eff  43.1% (staged) -> 49.5% (NVLink)   [ideal 50%]
     4-GPU eff  18.1% (staged) -> 24.2% (NVLink)   [ideal 25%]
  So NVLink moves the regime from "comm-bound, badly sub-linear" to "near-ideal,
  asymptotically pinned just under 1.0x". This CONFIRMS the M5b prediction's
  DIRECTION (cheaper comm via NVLink shrinks the gap to crossover) and QUANTIFIES
  it (the residual gap to 1.0x is now ~1% for 2-GPU, not the comm tax).

==========================================================================
HONEST FINDING (g5/g83): On a REAL NVLink node (4x A100-SXM4-40GB, NV12 bonds,
cudaDeviceCanAccessPeer=1 on all 12 pairs), 4-GPU data-parallel TRAINING is
CORRECT to the last bit (max|delta|=0, N=4) AND near-ideal in efficiency (2-GPU
eff -> 49.5%, 4-GPU eff -> 24.3%, vs the 50%/25% ceiling) — NVLink removes
almost the entire comm tax that crippled the staged-host node (43%/18% there).
BUT even on NVLink the per-step training speedup does NOT cross 1.0x in the
swept range: 2-GPU asymptotes to 0.99x, 4-GPU to 0.974x. The reason is NOT the
comm (NVLink fixed that) — it is the MODEL: pure DATA parallelism shards only the
batch (B=64 rows over N ranks), but the per-step cost of an H->H MLP is the H^2
GEMM, which is WEIGHT-bound, not batch-bound, once H is large; sharding 64 rows
into 16 barely reduces per-rank FLOPs, so each rank does nearly the FULL 1-GPU
compute PLUS a (now-cheap) all-reduce. Data-parallel DDP therefore APPROACHES
but cannot EXCEED 1.0x for a single training step of this small-batch MLP, on
ANY transport. To actually BEAT 1-GPU you need either (a) a batch-bound regime
(much larger B so per-rank compute genuinely drops ~1/N — DDP's true use case is
THROUGHPUT at fixed large global batch, where N-GPU does N x the samples/sec),
or (b) MODEL/tensor parallelism (shard the H^2 weight, not the batch). This is a
crisp, measured negative on the per-step-wall axis and a strong positive on the
comm-efficiency axis: NVLink is the cure for the comm tax (eff -> ideal), but
data-parallel per-step wall is bounded by 1.0x by construction at small batch.

The DURABLE results: (1) the M4 DDP byte-eq invariant holds max|delta|=0 at N=4
on real NVLink P2P (correctness is transport-independent); (2) NVLink lifts DDP
efficiency to NEAR-IDEAL (the comm tax is essentially eliminated), measured A/B
on identical silicon; (3) the 1.0x ceiling for per-step data-parallel wall at
small batch is a structural property (weight-bound GEMM), not a transport defect.
