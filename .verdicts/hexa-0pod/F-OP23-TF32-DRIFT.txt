F-OP23-TF32-DRIFT — TF32 vs FP64 N-step training-TRAJECTORY drift (RTX 5070)
============================================================================
STATUS: GREEN — TF32-mode VALIDATED as a REAL training fast-mode, NOT a 1-step illusion.
        Over N=100 steps the TF32 trajectory's LOSS tracks the FP64 trajectory's loss to
        ~1e-7 (worst gap 2.5e-5, AND that worst gap is at step 1 — it does NOT grow with
        steps), the weight rel-RMS stays BOUNDED at ~5e-7 (actually SHRINKS as both lanes
        converge), and the TF32 trajectory is self-byte-eq run-to-run over the WHOLE
        trajectory (W max|delta|=0 AND loss max|delta|=0 at step N, not just step 1).
        4/4 cells (DEFAULT + PEDANTIC, D={768,1536}, B={1,8}) confirm it.

HARDWARE: aiden RTX 5070 (sm_120 / cc12.0, 12227 MiB), CUDA 13.0 (cuda_13.0.r13.0).
          FREE sidecar pool — NO vast, NO pod, NO leak. GPU idle-guarded before each timed
          run (util<5% & mem<800MiB); the guard held the run until the card was exclusive
          (a parallel agent shared the card). Remote workdir removed after the run.

QUESTION (the LARGER one OP-20 #2999 deferred): OP-20 proved a SINGLE TF32 flame step is
self-byte-eq run-to-run + rel-RMS ~1.13e-6 vs FP64 + 4.2x+ faster (breaks the ~3x FP64
cap). Its verdict flagged: "Multi-step drift of the TF32 trajectory vs FP64 is the (larger)
question, deferred." A 1-step rel-RMS of 1e-6 could be EITHER (a) a real fast-mode — drift
bounded, loss curves track over N steps — OR (b) a 1-step illusion — the TF32 trajectory
peels away from FP64 (loss diverges). OP-23 decides it.

HONEST FRAMING (g5): NN training is CHAOTIC. Even FP64-vs-FP64 with a 1-ULP perturbation
diverges in WEIGHTS over many steps (butterfly) while the LOSS stays equivalent. So the
RIGHT metric is "does TF32 LOSS track FP64 LOSS within training noise" (training-equivalent),
NOT "do the weights stay byte-close" (they won't, and that's fine — that is exactly why
flame's byte-exact identity is SELF-determinism, not cross-precision). Bounded loss-tracking
= TF32 is a real fast-mode; loss divergence = the 1-step number was an illusion.

HARNESS: tool/bench/flame_traj_drift_tf32_op23.cu — runs ONE CONTINUOUS trajectory per lane
(AdamW state W/m/v PERSISTS across steps so drift ACCUMULATES; OP-20's harness RESET every
step). Both lanes start from the SAME seed and consume the SAME fixed data every step. Step
DAG byte-identical to OP-20 (fwd GEMM -> fused valley LN+gelu+copy -> transpose-elim bwd
GEMM -> single-launch AdamW); only the cuBLAS compute type differs (TF32 = CUDA_R_32F /
COMPUTE_32F_FAST_TF32 tensor-op; FP64 = CUDA_R_64F / COMPUTE_64F). Added a deterministic
scalar LOSS = mean(G^2) over the post-valley activation (fixed-order block tree reduce, no
atomics). The TF32 trajectory is run TWICE to test self-byte-eq over the WHOLE trajectory.
DRIVER: tool/bench/run_op23_5070.sh (idle-guarded; DEFAULT + PEDANTIC).
N = 100 steps. Representative configs that fit + run on the 5070: D={768,1536}, T=256,
B={1,8}. Full raw log: tool/bench/op23_5070_raw.log.

============================ ANSWERS TO THE OP-23 QUESTIONS ================================
Q1. Does rel-RMS(TF32-W vs FP64-W) stay BOUNDED or grow unboundedly?
    BOUNDED. It starts ~1.13e-6 (the OP-20 1-step number), then SHRINKS to ~4.5-5.3e-7 by
    step 100 — it does NOT grow. The weights stay within ~5e-7 rel-RMS of FP64 across the
    whole trajectory. max|dW| does creep up (1e-8 -> ~5e-7) as the chaotic component slowly
    accumulates, but stays MICROSCOPIC (NN training is forgiving to ~1e-3 perturbations;
    we are 3-4 orders of magnitude inside that). This is the GOOD case (chaotic-but-small).

Q2. Do the LOSS curves track or peel apart?
    THEY TRACK — decisively. The TF32 loss matches the FP64 loss to ~1e-7 every step. The
    WORST per-step loss gap over the whole trajectory is 2.495e-5, and it occurs at STEP 1
    (the cold-start step before AdamW's running averages settle); from step 6 onward the
    gap DROPS to ~1e-7 and stays flat. There is NO peeling, NO accumulation, NO drift trend
    in the loss. The two trajectories converge to the SAME loss (~0.40739) along the SAME
    curve. Loss-tracking = YES at every config.

Q3. Is the TF32 trajectory self-byte-eq run-to-run at step N?
    YES — over the WHOLE 100-step trajectory, not just step 1. Two independent TF32 runs
    give W max|delta|=0.000e+00 AND per-step loss max|delta|=0.000e+00 at every cell.
    Determinism holds across the entire trajectory. PEDANTIC needed? NO — DEFAULT
    TF32-tensor-op is already bit-identical run-to-run (PEDANTIC gives the identical
    numbers), same as OP-20 found for the single step.

=============================== THE JUDGMENT CALL (g5) =====================================
TF32-as-real-fast-mode: VALIDATED (not a 1-step illusion).
  - LOSS-TRACKING (the decisive, training-equivalent metric): YES — ~1e-7 every step,
    worst 2.5e-5 at the cold-start step, no growth. The TF32 loss curve IS the FP64 loss
    curve within training noise.
  - WEIGHT DRIFT: BOUNDED ~5e-7 rel-RMS (shrinks, doesn't grow). Honestly: the weights are
    NOT byte-equal to FP64 and never will be (chaos) — but that was never the contract.
    flame's byte-exact identity is SELF-determinism (TF32-vs-TF32 = 0, confirmed over the
    whole trajectory), at a different PRECISION CONTRACT (W14: rel-rms <= 1e-2 vs same
    dtype), NOT cross-precision byte-equality.
  - DETERMINISM: TF32 trajectory self-byte-eq at step N (W and loss max|delta|=0). The
    reproducibility identity holds over the whole trajectory, not just one step.
  => OP-20's deterministic TF32 fast-mode is a REAL training fast-mode. The 1-step
     rel-RMS 1e-6 was NOT an illusion: it stays bounded and the loss tracks over N steps.
     TF32-mode is a legitimate flame product mode (deterministic + training-equivalent +
     >3x faster), confirmed at the TRAJECTORY level.

HONEST CAVEATS (g5):
  1. N=100 on a small synthetic config (D<=1536, fixed data, single GEMM+valley+AdamW step,
     no real dataset/LR-schedule/long-horizon). It proves the drift is BOUNDED and the loss
     TRACKS over 100 steps on this DAG; it does not prove a full multi-epoch real-corpus run
     (longer horizons + LR decay + larger D are the natural follow-on, GPU-time permitting).
     The drift TREND, however, is flat-to-shrinking out to step 100 — no sign of late blow-up.
  2. The worst loss gap is the cold-start step-1 transient, not a trajectory drift; this is
     expected (TF32 vs FP64 differ most before AdamW's bias-corrected moments settle).
  3. Same consumer-card caveat as OP-20: the speed magnitude (4.2x@B=1) is 5070-specific
     (throttled FP64). OP-23 measures DRIFT/CORRECTNESS, not speed — speed is OP-20's result.
  4. Loss here = mean(G^2) over the post-valley activation (a stable deterministic proxy),
     not a task cross-entropy; it is a faithful scalar to compare the two precision lanes'
     trajectories, which is what the drift question needs.
