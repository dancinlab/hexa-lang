WHAT CHANGED vs the OP-23 harness (flame_traj_drift_tf32_op23.cu -> _op23b.cu):
  (1) N = 500 (was 100) — 5x longer horizon.
  (2) LR SCHEDULE — lr(t) = base_lr * [linear warmup 50 steps 0->1, then cosine decay 1->0.05]
      (the standard transformer schedule). base_lr=0.001. Computed in DOUBLE on the host and
      passed IDENTICALLY to both lanes each step, so the schedule itself is NOT a source of
      cross-lane divergence — only the GEMM/AdamW math precision differs. lr samples (verbatim):
        t=1: 2.000000e-05   t=50(peak): 1.000000e-03   t=125: 9.363621e-04
        t=250: 6.074829e-04   t=500: 5.000000e-05
  (3) HARDER synthetic — the target dGrad is now a row/col-dependent sinusoidal STRUCTURE (was a
      flat pseudo-random fill) and default D bumped 768->1024, so the loss has real descent
      structure over 500 steps instead of flat noise.
The step DAG is otherwise byte-identical to OP-23/OP-20 (fwd GEMM -> fused valley LN+gelu+copy ->
transpose-elim bwd GEMM -> single-launch AdamW); only the cuBLAS compute type differs (TF32 =
CUDA_R_32F / COMPUTE_32F_FAST_TF32 tensor-op; FP64 = CUDA_R_64F / COMPUTE_64F), and AdamW's LR is
now per-step from the schedule. The TF32 trajectory is run TWICE for whole-trajectory self-byte-eq.

HONEST FRAMING (g5, carried verbatim from OP-23): NN training is CHAOTIC. Even FP64-vs-FP64 with
a 1-ULP perturbation diverges in WEIGHTS over many steps (butterfly) while the LOSS stays
equivalent. So the RIGHT metric is LOSS-TRACKING (training-equivalent), NOT weight byte-closeness
(chaos GUARANTEES the weights drift — that is exactly why flame's identity is SELF-determinism,
not cross-precision). Bounded loss-tracking = TF32 is a real fast-mode at this horizon; a
sustained/late loss divergence would mean the 1-step number was an illusion at N=500.

================================ THE THREE KEY QUESTIONS ANSWERED ===========================
Q1. Does loss-tracking stay BOUNDED to N=500, or does a LATE blow-up appear?
    BOUNDED. In every cell the WORST loss-tracking gap is at step 3-5 (the EARLY transient),
    not late: worstStep in {3,3,5,3}. The LATE-half (steps>250) worst loss-track is 3.24e-6 /
    2.16e-7 / 3.52e-6 / 3.24e-6 — i.e. SMALLER than the early worst and FLAT to step 500. The
    step-500 tracking is 1.1e-7 (B=8) to 3.4e-6 (B=1). NO late blow-up. The OP-23 caveat is
    resolved in the GOOD direction: the bounded tracking holds at 5x the horizon.

Q2. Does the LR SCHEDULE (varying step sizes, esp. the high-LR warmup peak) AMPLIFY drift?
    NO. The warmup-peak window [45..55] — where the LR hits its 1e-3 maximum — has a worst
    loss-track of 3.28e-6 / 2.91e-7 / 3.34e-6 / 3.28e-6, the SAME order of magnitude as the
    steady-state ~3e-6 (B=1) / ~2e-7 (B=8). The peak is NOT a spike. The schedule (computed in
    double, identical to both lanes) does not introduce extra cross-precision divergence; the
    only precision difference is in the GEMM/AdamW math, and that stays bounded across the whole
    LR sweep. Bounded-tracking SURVIVES the LR schedule.

Q3. Is the TF32 trajectory still self-byte-eq run-to-run at step 500?
    YES, every cell. Two independent TF32 trajectories from the same seed/data/schedule produce
    W max|delta|=0.000e+00 AND loss max|delta|=0.000e+00 over the WHOLE 500-step trajectory.
    flame's SELF-determinism identity holds at the longer horizon.

NOTE on weight rel-RMS (the chaotic, NON-decisive metric): at step 500 it is 9.7e-3 (D=1024,B=1)
/ 4.6e-5 (B=8) / 8.7e-3 (D=768,B=1) — LARGER than OP-23's ~5e-7-at-N=100 because (a) 5x more
steps for chaos to accumulate, (b) the harder structured target drives larger weight magnitudes,
and (c) B=1 is the noisiest regime (B=8 is ~200x tighter). This is EXACTLY the expected chaotic-
but-bounded behavior, and is precisely why the LOSS, not the weights, is the decisive metric. The
weights drift (butterfly); the loss tracks. That is the OP-23 lesson, re-confirmed at N=500.

==================================== HONEST VERDICT ========================================
TF32-FAST-MODE HOLDS AT THE LONGER / HARSHER HORIZON. Over N=500 steps under a standard
transformer LR schedule (linear warmup + cosine decay) on a harder structured synthetic, the
deterministic TF32 fast-mode's LOSS tracks the FP64 reference loss to ~3e-6 (B=1) / ~1e-7 (B=8)
with NO late blow-up and NO LR-schedule amplification, and the TF32 trajectory remains self-byte-
eq run-to-run over the whole 500-step trajectory. This STRENGTHENS OP-23: the 1-step rel-RMS of
~1e-6 was a real fast-mode, not a 1-step illusion, and the bound is now demonstrated at 5x the
horizon and through the high-LR warmup peak. The TF32 mode is training-equivalent (loss-tracking
bounded) up to at least N=500 on this proxy.

SYNTHETIC CAVEAT (unchanged from OP-23, honest): this is STILL a SYNTHETIC proxy — loss=mean(G^2)
over a single fused projection block with a structured synthetic target, NOT a real corpus and NOT
the full CLMConvMoE multi-layer trainer. The real-data end-to-end run is GPU-BUILD-gated
