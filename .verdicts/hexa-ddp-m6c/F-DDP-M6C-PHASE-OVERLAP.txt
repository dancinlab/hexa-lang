F-DDP-M6C-PHASE-OVERLAP — re-pipeline the M6 cross-node ring to OVERLAP its two
phases, beating the M6b RTT-bound throughput ceiling.

  HONEST prediction (g5): WAN stays RTT-dominated. Full-duplex (send||recv)

OVERLAP TECHNIQUE (what changed — MATH IDENTICAL to M6):
  stdlib/ddp/m6c_overlap/ring_perf_m6c.c reuses the EXACT M1/M3/M5/M6/M6b ring
  schedule (2(N-1) steps, chunk_start partition, parity ordering) and the SAME
  host-memory FP64 reduce (x[i]+=stage[i], unchanged accumulation ORDER => FP64
  byte-eq preserved). ONLY the per-step transport's blocking discipline changes,
  selectable by --mode to isolate each effect on the SAME path:
    * serial   — exact M6b: blocking send-THEN-recv, parity-ordered (baseline).
    * duplex   — (A) FULL-DUPLEX: a dedicated SENDER THREAD pushes this step's
                 outbound chunk while the MAIN THREAD simultaneously pulls the
                 inbound chunk. Send and recv proceed concurrently on the two TCP
                 half-duplex directions instead of being serialized. Collapses
                 the per-phase cost from (send-latency + recv-latency) ~= 2 RTT
                 toward max(one-way) ~= 1 RTT per all-reduce.
    * pipeline — (A)+(B): full-duplex AND each step's chunk split into P
                 sub-chunks (--pchunks P) streamed so multiple are in flight,
                 keeping TCP's pipe full across the bandwidth-delay product once
                 the message is large enough to be transfer-bound.
  The reduce runs AFTER the inbound chunk is fully received for that step — same
  byte ordering as M6/M6b, so the overlap is transport-only and cannot perturb
  the FP64 result.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RTT-COUNT PER ALL-REDUCE & RESIDUAL FLOOR
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  N=2 all-reduce = reduce-scatter (1 phase) + all-gather (1 phase) = 2 phases.
    * serial : each phase = blocking send-THEN-recv = ~1 RTT/phase => ~2 RTT.
               measured small-S floor 0.41s ≈ 2 x 205ms. ✓
    * duplex/pipeline : each phase overlaps send||recv = ~max(one-way) per
               phase; the two phases still chain (all-gather depends on
               reduce-scatter's result) => ~1 RTT total round-trip dependency.
               measured small-S floor 0.205-0.22s ≈ 1 x 205ms. ✓
  RESIDUAL FLOOR = ~1 RTT (~205 ms) per all-reduce. This is the HARD limit:
  the all-gather phase cannot begin until the reduce-scatter result returns, so
  at least one round-trip of latency is irreducible for a 2-node ring. Overlap
  took the count from ~2 RTT to ~1 RTT (the maximum possible reduction for 2
  phases) — it CANNOT go below 1 RTT without changing the algorithm (e.g. a
  recursive-halving / tree collective, or hiding comm behind compute). Phase-
  overlap is therefore near-OPTIMAL for THIS schedule, and ~2x is the real, full
  win, not a partial one.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HONEST VERDICT (g5)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Re-pipelining the M6 WAN ring to OVERLAP its two phases (full-duplex
  send||recv, optionally + chunk pipelining) is byte-exact (max|Δ|=0, FP64,
  all boundary + large cases, all modes) and ~2.0x faster than the M6b serial-
  phase baseline across the whole latency-bound regime — exactly the predicted
  factor, because the ring paid 2 serialized RTTs per all-reduce and duplexing
  collapses that to 1. In the bandwidth regime (>=16-32MB) 32-way chunk
  pipelining adds a further lift (up to 2.40x over serial, 1.26x over duplex) by
  keeping the pipe full. The win is genuine but BOUNDED: the residual ~1-RTT
  (~205ms) round-trip dependency is an irreducible floor for a 2-node ring, so
  phase-overlap meaningfully beats the M6b ceiling (halves the per-all-reduce
  RTT count, the maximum possible for a 2-phase collective) WITHOUT defeating
  the fundamental WAN latency. This is commodity-WAN-TCP perf reality, NOT an
  RDMA/datacenter number, and the byte-eq guarantee (M6) is untouched. Practical
  takeaway: cross-country synchronous DDP over this transport gets ~2x cheaper
  per all-reduce from phase-overlap, but stays latency-dominated — useful only
  when compute >> communication.
