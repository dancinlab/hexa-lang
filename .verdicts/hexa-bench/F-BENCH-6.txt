whole step replays as ONE graph launch, amortizing per-op launch+sync overhead, and

The residual ~2x is INVARIANT under graph capture. flame's per-step wall does NOT move

WHERE THE RESIDUAL ~2x LIVES = GEMM-THROUGHPUT, not launch.
  The step time is dominated by the two cuBLAS-TF32 GEMMs (fwd H=A@W, bwd dW=A^T@dG).
  The elementwise kernels (valley LN+gelu, transpose, AdamW) are tiny relative to the
  GEMMs, and the per-op LAUNCH cost (the thing graph-capture removes) is ~1% of the
  step. torch is faster on the SAME nominal cuBLAS-TF32 path because torch's inductor
  selects better-tuned GEMM algorithms/heuristics + its captured/fused elementwise for
  THIS small consumer card (sm_120) shape — i.e. the gap is GEMM kernel-selection /
  throughput, which graph capture cannot touch. This is exactly the closed-neg BENCH-3
  flagged as the likely honest outcome.

CONTRAST with HEXA-FLAME-FAST (whole-step megakernel/graph-capture closed-neg ~1.0x for
the FULL interpreted FP64 trainer): there the wall was interpreted per-step glue +
occupancy. Here the wall is DIFFERENT (compiled minimal C++ step), and graph capture is
STILL ~1.0x — but for the OPPOSITE reason: not interpreter glue, but because the cuBLAS
GEMM already dominates and launch overhead is negligible. Two different walls, same
graph-capture verdict (~1.0x), both honestly measured.

================================ CONCLUSION ==========================================
- Graph-capture step/s vs eager: 0.98-1.01x at B=1,2,4,8 (NO speedup; launch overhead
  is ~1% of the step wall, in the noise).
- bit-eq graph-vs-eager: YES (max|delta(W')|=0 all B) · determinism: YES (=0 all B).
- The residual ~2.0-2.4x vs torch is GEMM-THROUGHPUT (cuBLAS algo-selection on sm_120 vs
  torch inductor), NOT launch/glue/occupancy. BENCH-3's launch attribution is REFUTED;
  the residual is pinned to GEMM. The lever to close it would be a better-tuned own/cuBLAS
  GEMM algo for this shape on sm_120 — NOT megakernel/graph-capture.
